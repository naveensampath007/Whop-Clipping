# HardScope Episode 3: V3 edits and clipping research

These three revisions give each scene a clear opening question, enough context to follow it, and a comic reveal. They use the campaign’s approved Episode 3 footage. Full animated dialogue captions remain a later stage.

## What changed

- **More partners, more pets — about 19 seconds.** Starts with the agreement about other partners, explains the response of bringing home more pets, and lands on the **15 cats** reveal.
- **Dolls in court — about 20.5 seconds.** Keeps the scene chronological: the claim about a room and dolls, the judge’s question, the pocket and physical reveal, then the **30 dolls** punchline.
- **A better provider — about 17.2 seconds.** Opens directly on “Why don’t you get…,” establishes the provider objection, reveals the salary, and ends on “Let’s talk.”

Each has a short factual opening text hook for approximately 2.25 seconds: **“MORE PARTNERS. / MORE PETS.”**, **“VOODOO DOLLS… / IN COURT?”**, or **“A BETTER / PROVIDER?”**

Framing follows the speaker or relevant evidence. Stacked shots appear only where seeing both subjects helps explain the exchange; the provider edit uses no stack. An audio timing error that caused silence at the start of the earlier dolls edit has been corrected.

## What the research supports

[YouTube’s Shorts discussion with Jenny Hoyos and Todd Sherman](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) emphasizes hooking attention immediately and telling a compact mini-story. For these edits, that means an audible specific premise, a question worth answering, and a reveal that answers it.

[Instagram’s watch-time guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/) includes a comedy creator describing tests of different hooks and opening lines. The useful lesson is to test the opening against actual audience behavior.

Avoid generic show introductions, silent lead-ins, repetitive setup, random cuts, unrelated background footage, and promises the ending does not fulfill. Preserve the dialogue’s meaning and enough reaction timing for the joke to land. Duration should follow the story; extra seconds must add context or payoff.

## Comparable Reels reviewed

The [official Zach Justice Episode 1 promo](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeGDVS1j5r7/) briefly stacks the two partners around 4.7–6 seconds while introducing their disagreement, then returns to individual speakers and evidence. Its amount reveal is followed by a reaction. This supports using framing to explain the scene.

An [independent Episode 1 clip](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dd-kWOUPF76/) spends its opening on a show introduction and keeps unrelated gameplay below the program. Those choices were avoided here. Neither example’s public counts establish that its editing caused success or failure. No comparison footage, audio, or graphics are included in these exports.

## What to check after posting

Compare available stayed-to-watch versus swiped-away figures, average watch time, completion, and the retention curve. Look for early exits, drops during setup, and whether viewers reach the reveal. Replays can indicate enjoyment or confusion, so inspect the surrounding moment. See [YouTube’s retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en).

Use those results to improve the next opening. Campaign eligibility, qualified views, audience geography, and manager approval still determine payment; these edits cannot guarantee views or payouts.
